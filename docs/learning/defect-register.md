# Defect register

## 2026-11-08 — The 3D mechanism follows poses instead of connected mechanics (unresolved in that build; see the note of 2026-11-11)

The owner required the 3D typewriter to follow the actual mechanism. Audit of 4a71edf confirmed that rendered keylevers change length, typebars follow interpolated orientations, escapement dogs follow carriage state, and ink comes from a normalized-angle threshold. Existing state-level tests do not establish rigid joint closure, geometric contact or a causal force path through the visible parts.

The comprehensive repair plan and evidence-gated progress board are in docs/work/5_connected-mechanism/plan.md. Planned regression coverage includes joint/length invariants, geometric ink contact and registration, energy and timestep convergence, component-removal/blocking counterfactuals, and common solver states for real typing and the tutorial. The no-loss input buffer and permanent stored ink remain acceptance requirements. Planning is complete; the defect is not fixed and implementation tests have not yet been added.

**2026-11-11, after the rebuild.** The build this entry describes was replaced on `main` by one made from scratch around a physical simulation of the mechanism (see the devlog). In it every drawn part takes its position from a simulated body, and links and springs are drawn between their end points' live positions (`AGENTS.md`, Invariants). Tests for the class, in `tests/keystroke.test.ts`: "the wire links stay rigid and the visible linkage matches independent kinematics" (joint closure and link length); "a keystroke happens in causal order and prints one letter one space wide"; "the print needs momentum: a slow press brings the type to the paper without inking it" and "counterfactuals: each part of the chain is necessary for its effect" (ink only from contact, and a part taken out breaks only what it causes); "energy: the finger powers the typebar, the mainspring powers the carriage"; "results converge as the timestep is refined". The guided tour replays the same simulation that typing uses. Of the two standing requirements: no typed input is lost (`tests/typing.test.ts`); ink is permanent on the sheet and survives the tour, but this build does not save sheets between page loads, which the earlier one did.

## 2026-11-07 — The tutorial named parts without teaching the mechanism

The owner reported that the exploded tour did not leave them able to teach someone else how a typewriter works. The opening animation only separated assemblies, while later chapters relied on broad 3D views and static paragraphs. Connections, contact and the distinction between finger energy and carriage-spring energy were not legible.

Replaced the default presentation with animated causal close-ups, a complete opening stroke, synchronized cause/effect captions, selectable moments and explanation questions. The 3D exploded view remains optional spatial context. Regression coverage in tests/tutorial.test.ts requires the opening's selected moments to demonstrate motion before ink, contact before carriage travel, exactly one space, and permanent ink after reset; all selectable moments must match their narrated interval. Headless visual acceptance additionally checks that the key linkage, ink-contact layers, escapement hold and return motions are readable on desktop and mobile. See docs/work/4_causal-tutorial/plan.md for the rendered verification and independent review.

**2026-11-11, after the rebuild.** The tutorial described here went with the earlier build. Its replacement is a tour of 18 steps, each of which replays the real simulation in slow motion and stops at the moment its text describes (the escapement tripping before the letter prints, the mainspring paying for the carriage's step, and so on). Check for the class: `scripts/walk-tour.sh` walks every step in a browser and fails if one does not finish or never reaches its moment; the order of events and the split between finger and spring energy are asserted in `tests/keystroke.test.ts`. Not carried over from the earlier tutorial: the explanation questions. Automatic playback and camera moves are not switched off for reduced-motion settings, and the narrow-screen layout has not been checked on a phone.

## 2026-11-07 — Fast input was lost while the mechanism moved

The user reported missed letters during fast typing. Keyboard and pointer events went directly into the mechanical model, which rejected presses during carriage movement, paper feed, or a typebar's return; overlapping presses could jam before any text printed. A 5 ms interval burst reproduced the issue with zero printed letters and a jam.

Added a separate ordered input buffer that waits for a complete physical cycle, snapshots character modifiers and settings at input time, and retains controls in sequence. Hard-margin overflow stays pending until a manual control or fresh sheet makes space. A visible pending count and unload warning protect unprinted input. The original mechanical collision behavior remains covered independently.

Regression coverage: tests/buffered-typewriter.test.ts exercises zero-delay bursts, repeated and overlapping keys, spaces and controls, case/ribbon/force snapshots, typing during returns, margin recovery, new sheets, slow motion, and input release without cancellation. Browser verification exercises real keyboard events against the rendered app. See docs/work/1_buffered-typing/plan.md for delivery evidence.

**2026-11-11, after the rebuild.** In the build now on `main`, typed input waits in a queue (`src/sim/typist.ts`) until the mechanism can take it, and the status line says how many keystrokes are waiting. Tests for the class, in `tests/typing.test.ts`: "a burst typed all at once is printed in order with no letter lost"; "the same key struck repeatedly prints every time"; "input arriving at random moments is never dropped, reordered or stalled"; "capitals waiting at a full line: Shift is let go, the machine rests, and they print after the return"; and `scripts/smoke.sh` for real key events in a browser. Not carried over: the warning when leaving the page with unprinted input.
