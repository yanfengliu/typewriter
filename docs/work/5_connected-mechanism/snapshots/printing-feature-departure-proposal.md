## Why directional departure needs a proof

advanceEndpoint immediately reports any guard <=0. Thus a reverse guard at an exact departing tie would fire again at the same state. Omitting it without a verified one-way interval could miss a reversal. The proposed solution is a finite monotone departure interval derived from physical bounds, not a time epsilon. It is permissible only if all conditions below are established before omitting that one guard. All other feature/contact guards remain active, the outgoing smooth domain is enforced, and the omitted guard is restored at the first strictly interior accepted state. Failure to obtain representable interior progress ends this investigation; there is no interval search or alternate constant.

For a link closure let d=D dot T be its nonzero denominator, where D is input point minus follower point and T is the follower tangent. With input derivative bounds P1 and P2, follower tangent bound T0, axis norm n, global finite D bound D0, and d_min=abs(d_at_boundary)/2, use:

- B1=D0*P1/d_min for the follower angle derivative.
- D1=P1+T0*B1 and denominator derivative bound C1=P1*T0+B1*T0*T0+D0*n*T0*B1.
- B2=(D1*D1+D0*P2+D0*n*T0*B1*B1)/d_min for the second angle derivative.
- Restrict the local coordinate interval to radius <=abs(d_at_boundary)/(2*C1), maintaining the denominator bootstrap. Recursively apply these bounds to the three serial closures, starting with key angle derivatives 1 and 0. Bound rotating-point derivatives by T0*B1 and T0*(n*B1*B1+B2).

Use finite vector norm bounds for D0 and rotating arms, not an assumption that an arbitrary trial exactly satisfies the rod length. The chosen closeLink assembly branch and unwrap selection must also retain their current strict margins over the interval; a denominator bound alone does not exclude a branch-selector jump.
