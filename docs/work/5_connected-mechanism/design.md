# Royal standard mechanism reference

Status: research specification complete; geometric reconstruction and implementation remain unverified

Date: 2026-11-08 UTC

## Reference decision and evidence

Use the **Royal standard segment-shift mechanism depicted in TM 10-602 / TO 25-5-34, with its pica spacing option**. The title page identifies the Departments of the Army and the Air Force and the date 6 January 1955. Paragraph 3 explicitly identifies the standard machine as segment shift and says carriage length varies between machines. The inspected cover/introduction do not identify a commercial KMM, KMG or HH model. Do not attach one of those model names to this reconstruction or claim a measured replica of a particular serial number.

Source: [Royal Typewriter Maintenance and Repair, January 1955](https://site.xavier.edu/polt/typewriters/Royal1955.pdf). This is the government technical manual itself, hosted by Richard Polt at Xavier University, rather than a modern account of its mechanism. Provenance was checked against the cover and title page. No assertion about the rights of third-party material elsewhere on that website is needed.

The inspected download has 72 PDF pages, 7,591,792 bytes and SHA-256 `26704307fcb209fd4cca2f09343e153beb39545d6f8fb6068e51daf5cd9e5c4e`. In this file, printed page 1 is PDF page 2. All PDF page references below are one-based. The file is scanned; text extraction returned no usable text. The relevant pages were rendered with PDFium and read visually, including enlarged figure 7. The web reader timed out; the complete downloaded file, rather than indexed snippets, supplied the findings below.

**Go:** this manual supplies a coherent family, component connections, mechanical event order, assembly drawings, and several dimensional checks sufficient to begin a labelled engineering reconstruction. **No-go:** it does not supply a full dimensioned production drawing set, measured masses, spring rates, contact properties, or exact performance traces. M1 research is not proof that a fitted mechanism closes, conserves energy, prints correctly, or matches a particular surviving Royal. Those are M2 onward gates.

## Coordinate and topology contract

Describe front/rear and left/right from the typist's position. Ordinary typing moves the carriage physically **left**, placing successive marks to the right in the paper's coordinates. Shift moves the **segment and its typebar pivots down** for capitals; it does not lift the carriage. The platen and paper travel laterally together with the carriage. Rotation about the platen axis advances paper independently of lateral carriage motion.

Numbers in the following key-action chain refer to **figure 7**, not global part numbers. The manual's paragraph 28 explicitly gives this drive path:

`finger → key lever 25 → key lever link 26 → front link 27 → typebar lever link 28 → typebar lever 29 → typebar link 30 → typebar → typehead/ribbon/paper/platen`

The key lever pivots at its rear support; depression pulls link 26 down. Front link 27 pivots down and forward and pulls link 28 forward. Link 28 pulls lever 29 forward. Lever 29 pulls link 30 and swings the typebar toward the cylinder. Figure 7 shows frame fulcrums for the key lever, front link and typebar lever. Typebars turn on the segment wire: paragraph 72 describes withdrawing that wire to remove bars and their corresponding links, and warns not to interchange them. Key lever spring 24 supplies return bias. The typebar and the typebar link are separate parts.

Lever 29 also drives a branch: `lever 29 → universal-bar link 23 → universal bar 18 → trip plate 20 contacts trip roll 22 → escapement rocker 8`. Link spring 21, individual trip adjustment 19 and trip screw 31 are part of this branch. The universal bar is a suspended pivoting assembly, supported by the mainspring bracket extension and adjustable bracket (figure 8); it is not a generic bar the typehead happens to strike near the platen. A key's trip adjustment may be changed by forming its link 23.

**Joint certainty:** the named connection order and illustrated fulcrums are documented. Figure 7 is a schematic assembly drawing, not a dimensioned joint drawing. It shows an oblong opening in front link 27 without explaining its functional travel; the precise pin/hook/sliding behavior and clearances of the small key-action connections are not fully specified by the accompanying text. Do not silently turn that opening into a known cam law or assert that every visible opening is a lost-motion slot. Initially treating an unresolved connection as an ideal hinge is a *reconstruction assumption* that must be labelled and tested through the entire stroke in both shift positions. If closure requires invented link stretching or an unexplained extra degree of freedom, the single-key prototype fails; inspect the real connection rather than hide that failure.

**Confirmed unilateral contacts and lost motion must remain contacts:** trip plate to trip roll; ribbon throw screw to lift lever; lift-lever stud to the shaped vibrator-link heel; shift-link elongated hole to shift-arm stud; segment lock to lock plate; key, rocker and shift stops; dogs/pawls/rolls to teeth. A fixed-length bar across any of these interfaces would remove the required clearance or overconstrain the mechanism.

## Escapement: required event order

The source describes a **loose dog, escapement roll and check pawl**, not two interchangeable symmetric dogs. Use its actual parts and sequence. Figure 7 part 2 is the escapement wheel, 8 the rocker, 12 the loose dog, 13 the escapement roll, and 3 the check pawl. Paragraphs 28 and 31 establish:

1. At rest the spring-loaded carriage loads the rack/pinion/wheel. The loose dog holds a wheel tooth. The wheel's load pushes the loose dog against the rocker drop screw; its own lighter spring biases it toward its loose-dog stop when unloaded.
2. A key drives the universal bar and trip plate. The plate bears on the trip roll, moving the lower rocker forward and upper rocker rearward.
3. The tooth slips off the loose dog and contacts the escapement roll. **Paragraph 31a(3)(d) adjusts the trip to occur when the typehead is 3/8 inch from the cylinder. This is 9.525 mm before cylinder contact.** A claim that the selected Royal does not release anything until after impact is contradicted by this adjustment.
4. The roll restrains the tooth while the rocker is displaced. The typebar completes its approach and produces contact with the supported sheet and raised ribbon. The exact small wheel motion during handoff must follow reconstructed tooth/contact geometry.
5. As the rocker returns, the roll releases the tooth. That tooth meets the check pawl, which pivots to let it pass.
6. The next wheel tooth is arrested by the loose dog, completing a single trip. Rocker return spring 32 and kickback spring 15 participate in resetting; front/rear limit plates bound its travel.

The manual gives a 1/64-inch (0.396875 mm) loose-dog/escapement-roll clearance at the specified adjustment, and discusses reducing it to reduce recoil under a held-heavy key. Paragraph 31d and figure 7 identify the washer's **axial** surface clearance; converting it into a tangential wheel-phase offset is incorrect. It is **not a measured half-column carriage movement**. The manual does not establish a positive minimum tangential backlash or preprint paper travel. A zero-backlash nominal reconstruction must be explicitly labelled an idealization and tested separately from finite-gap sensitivity. Do not label this exact mechanism “full drop” or “half drop” on the basis of an unrelated generic diagram. The documented handoff sequence and precontact trip are the specification; the displacement fraction and interval of stationary paper require a solved trace. A no-smear contact interval is an implementation acceptance check, not a historical timing assertion already established here.

**Rear-rocker clearance:** printed page 22, paragraph 31b(1)(b)/(2)(b), requires slight clearance from the rear limit plate when the typehead is held against the cylinder. Rocker rear-stop contact before print is therefore not a valid proxy for successful tooth-to-roll transfer. Keep the rear stop, roll-hold contact and ribbon-cam contact distinct. The independent [coupled-mechanism review](reviews/4_implementation.md) rejects the prototype's combined early-stop fit; source agreement is reopened until these constraints are implemented and verified.

The rack transfers spring tension from the carriage to the escapement. Figure 9 has rack 7 on pivoted rack bail 4, pinion 9, wheel 12, pinion pawl 14 and pawl spring 13. During leftward typing travel, the pinion drives the pawl mounted on the wheel and therefore the wheel. During carriage return the pinion turns the other way and lifts the pawl out of engagement; **the pinion may turn without rotating the escapement wheel** (paragraph 32c). A rigid always-engaged pinion-to-wheel gear constraint would be wrong. The rocker check pawl prevents backlash in return, backspace and tabulation.

The two rack-bail springs keep the rack in mesh; rack release or Tab lifts it out. Specified released clearance is 1/16 inch. Pica and elite rack/pinion/wheel components differ and are not interchangeable. Select **pica: 10 characters/inch = 2.54 mm per settled character** (paragraph 32). This pitch is a geometric ratchet result, not permission to clamp the carriage to a software column target.

Space uses a separate lever/link/trip-arm path into the escapement rocker (figure 10, paragraph 33), without moving a typebar. Its idle trip-arm clearance is 0.008–0.010 inch; the manual specifies another 1/8 inch of spacebar travel after the first trip. Space does not require a fake invisible typebar strike.

## Ribbon: distinguish lift from transport

**Lift** is key/universal-bar powered. Figure 12 and paragraph 44 give: universal bar's throw-adjusting screw 9 contacts lift lever 7; its stud 8 presses the shaped heel of vibrator link 22; that link rotates vibrator arm 5 about support 6; the front of the arm raises vibrator 1. The vibrator is cotter-pinned to the arm and guided vertically by two extensions surrounding the type guide. Spring 23 returns the arm. The vibrator arm contacts the color-selection cam at the same time the type strikes paper; a cam reached too soon chokes the typebar, according to the manual. Thus this branch can physically load the key action and is not an independent decorative lift.

**Parent correction, independently reviewed in [review22](reviews/22_implementation.md):** the complete Royal1955 figure8 and factory UB drawings support a hinge carried on the moving universal-bar upper web, rather than the grounded hinge in the rejected reconstruction. The central ear is not individually numbered, so this remains a strong drawing-based inference. Use a relative lift angle and recompute moving-pivot translation, gravity and cross inertia. The UB-carried screw and hinge share a parent: at fixed relative angle their internal gap is invariant under UB motion. A screw already in compression can carry stroke load; an open gap cannot be closed merely by rotating their common parent. The later [Royal470 factory guide](https://site.xavier.edu/polt/typewriters/Royal470field.pdf), printed10/PDF12 frame36, identifies the screw's play adjustment but does not establish its initial gap for every setting.

Royal1955 section44b(2) documents spring23 as an extension spring from the rear of arm5 to the top of its fixed support6. Model its endpoint distance and tension if constructing the actual spring; a fitted angular stiffness is only a reduced approximation. A separate horizontal coil is visible near the upper lift mounting in Ames1968 RO-18, with apparent endpoints at a channel tab and lift lug. Those endpoint owners, function, preload and rate remain unverified. A declared inferred UB-to-lift spring can be evaluated as a reconstruction hypothesis; it must not be called historically established, omitted without consequence, or replaced by an invented UB-to-ground return spring. Revised candidate4 requires an explicit relative-state and restraint contract before geometry or force acceptance.

The color cam and bichrome lever change which part of the link heel the stud drives and limit the arm's stroke. Black raises the upper ribbon half to the typeface; red raises farther to place the lower half there; stencil keeps the ribbon retracted. The rest adjustment puts the ribbon top 1/16 inch above the type guide's flat top, with segment unshifted. Preserve these separate states and the return spring; do not infer lift solely from typebar angle.

**Transport** is powered by the mainspring barrel gear, not a ratchet advanced by each key. Paragraph 34 explicitly says ribbon feeds during leftward carriage motion from typing, Space, Tab or manual carriage release. Figure 11 shows the barrel gear driving a compound wormshaft pinion; its small pinion acts through a spring pawl in the pinion case. Rightward carriage return makes this pawl slip, leaving the wormshaft stationary (paragraph 35b). A worm on the shaft drives either the left spool's spur gear or an idler/feed-shaft/bevel-gear path to the right spool. The unused spool pays out under light drag. Shaft springs resist backlash and keep the ribbon wound tightly.

**Reversal** is a physical state change, not an arbitrary timer. The depleted spool's spring trip arm is released by the ribbon winding/end condition and contacts an automatic reverse arm. One side uses the cross-machine reverse shaft and lever. This puts a roller in the reverse cam's path; the rotating cam moves the front wormshaft up or down to change which gear it meshes with. A universal joint near the barrel allows that motion; a detent retains either position. The worm ratchet/pawl prevents reverse rotation during return. Manual reversal acts on the same selecting mechanism (paragraphs 39–41). Tooth counts, spool radii, ribbon thickness, feed length per step and cam dimensions are not fully dimensioned here and must be reconstructed/estimated explicitly.

## Shift, contact, paper and return

**Shift (figure 16, paragraphs 58–62):** shift key lever 9 pivots and pulls shift-frame arm 6 forward. Shift lock link 4 withdraws segment lock 3 from beneath lock plate 2. Shift link 5 has an explicitly described elongated hole: the link first takes up clearance, then its back edge meets the stud on shift arm 16. The arm rotates shift rod 15, moves balance-spring hook arms 14 downward and pulls arms 17, lowering the segment. The manual requires the lock to clear *before* downward segment movement. Segment rails with ball bearings/pinions constrain the motion; paired stops and cushions set each endpoint; balance springs 26 return the basket. Shift lock mechanically retains the depressed key. One-sixteenth inch of key overtravel after segment arrest allows release of shift lock. Do not move typeheads alone or translate the basket without solving the attached links.

**Impact alignment (figures 17–18, paragraph 62):** the typehead has a curved printing surface matched to the cylinder. The source specifies a round cylinder diameter of **1.486 inches = 37.7444 mm**. With one sheet inserted, the typebar should meet the segment abutment ring at the same time the typehead reaches the cylinder through the sheet. This ring limits overtravel, protecting ribbon and cylinder. Upper/lower stop adjustment aligns both character faces to one baseline. Use this as a geometric contact and stop condition, not an angle threshold. Exact face spacing, curvature tolerance, ribbon compression, sheet thickness and rubber stiffness are not supplied.

**Paper support (figure 6, paragraphs 25–26):** the platen backs the impact; the paper pan guides the entering sheet around it; a large rear feed roll holds and feeds the paper to three smaller front rolls; the paper bail holds it flush near the writing line. Equal roller pressure matters; the text explicitly describes loss of traction and paper slip. The release lever turns a pinion/release rod, camming the support arms away so the rolls drop from the cylinder and paper can move freely. A no-slip paper model is an explicit normal-condition approximation, valid only while rolls grip and support exists.

**Line feed (figure 4, paragraphs 21–22):** the return/line-space lever drives a pivoted pawl through its link. The pawl travels along a selector shield until it drops into the ratchet, then turns the platen until the lever reaches its stop. The detent roll seats between teeth; a second stop limits excessive ratchet motion. Selector positions admit 1, 2 or 3 lines. The standard ratchet has **28 teeth**, one revolution per 28 single spaces. The documented diameter yields approximately **4.235 mm of sheet travel per tooth**, close to one-sixth inch, under no slip. Use the selected ratchet geometry, not an unrelated fixed pixel feed increment.

**Carriage return:** hand work translates the carriage to the right against drawband tension, winding the spring barrel, while the escapement pinion freewheels relative to its wheel and ribbon feed clutch slips. The line-space lever's separate rotation indexes the platen. Normal operation can coordinate those two hand actions, but a bare horizontal return is not a causal reason for platen rotation. Keep their work and motion paths distinct.

## Other controls needed for the same family

**Backspace (figure 19, paragraphs 63–66):** the key lever pivots a lift lever; it raises a guided pawl into the carriage rack and the swinging arm drives it rightward one space. A stop attached to the pawl holds the escapement wheel while the pinion reverses. A rack lock keeps the bail meshed. The physical carriage and paper move; existing ink does not disappear. The specified wheel-stop/tooth clearance is 1/32 inch with the key depressed. This is not reverse ordinary escapement stepping.

**Tab (figure 13, paragraphs 45–48):** key → link → link lever/lift-arm rod → lift lever raises the rack out of the pinion. Another link advances the tab finger into the path of set stops on the carriage's tab rack. The same lift puts a brake gear into the carriage rack. That gear initially turns freely for 7–8 spaces, then a lug couples it to a friction hub, limiting carriage speed; its spring restores the gear after the operation. A tab stop catches the finger. Rack reengagement finishes final registration: the manual's adjustment test describes one-third space motion while held and the remaining two-thirds upon release after a one-space backspace. Do not leave the normal rack/pinion constraint active during tab travel or use a smooth target tween as the brake.

**Margins/bell/lock (figure 15, paragraphs 52–57):** adjustable spring-retained stops on the carriage meet a central stop locator. The right stop has a small arm that trips the bell about 7–8 spaces before arrest. At the limit, the line-lock lever drives crank link → crank → line-lock link → bail under key-lever heels, physically blocking descent; an extension also limits the universal bar. Margin release pivots the center post out of the stop's path so the carriage can pass without changing the stored margin position. Simplified bell acoustics may be synthesized from that contact, but not triggered by a disconnected count of keyboard events.

## Reconstruction bounds and verification contract

| Quantity or behavior | Evidence category | Required treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Named connectivity, shift direction, trip and handoff order, ribbon power source, clutches | Documented in cited text/figures | Preserve these causal paths and contact states |
| Pica pitch, platen diameter, ratchet tooth count, cited service clearances | Documented service values | Convert to SI and distinguish clearance from paper travel |
| Pivot coordinates, individual key/link lengths, typebar fan planes, tooth profiles and ratios not stated in text | Reconstructed | Fit from diagrams with uncertainty; prove closure, compatibility and alignment, including shift |
| Mass/inertia, spring constants/preload, friction, restitution, damping, rubber compliance | Estimated | Record units and plausible ranges; test energy accounting and sensitivity; do not call measured |
| Ink pressure/opacity, ribbon condition and sheet bending model | Macroscopic approximation | State model; require geometric supported contact and permanent sheet coordinates |
| Buffered input and finite virtual finger | Product abstraction | Keep outside the physical machine; explain that real Royal machines have no input queue |

A valid first reconstruction may simplify screw threads, bearing internals, tooth fillets, hidden sheet fibers, and cosmetic casing. It may represent a one-way clutch with a unilateral constraint and normal paper feed with inextensible no-slip arc length. Those simplifications must retain the correct bodies, transmission direction, available degrees of freedom, contacts, stored energy and work. It may not stretch a rigid link to close a gap, assign an unrelated phase to each mesh, transport ink without contact, or use software column targets as the physical arrest mechanism.

Minimum source-derived checks: key drive disconnected before actuation leaves its typebar unpowered; a blocked ribbon-lift branch loads/stops the key path; absent ribbon prevents ink but not impact; the segment unlocks before descending; both shift states close all links and place the selected face on one writing line; precontact escapement trip hands the tooth to the roll; roll return permits passage past check pawl and next-tooth arrest; removing spring pull prevents passive carriage travel; locked wheel prevents ordinary spacing while return may still freewheel its pinion; Space releases escapement without type impact; leftward carriage motion drives ribbon feed even without a print; rightward return does not unwind the feed train; tab disengages the rack before free travel; backspace preserves impressions; line feed is driven by its pawl/ratchet and paper material travel follows platen rotation while gripped.

**What this deliverable proves:** one coherent reference family and variant choice; the cited mechanism topology and service sequence; concrete contradictions to independent key/ribbon/carriage timelines; verified source identity, hash and page map.

**What it does not prove yet:** exact small-joint geometry, every dimension of the historical machine, a successful full-stroke/shift solution, numerical stability or energy fidelity, visible mesh/contact agreement, paper/ink accuracy, buffer preservation, teaching effectiveness, browser/mobile performance, or deployed behavior. In particular, the ambiguous small key connections and wheel displacement during dog-to-roll transfer remain reconstruction checks rather than established measurements.

## Fitted action prototype parameters

The connected prototype retains the documented four-rotor/three-rod topology. Key hinges run across the keyboard; fitted front-link and typebar-lever axes fan with the segment. Ideal spherical rod ends represent unresolved hook side play. These are reconstruction choices, not documented Royal production axes. Per-row fixed key-lever arm geometry gives approximately 10 mm finger travel. Rods are ideal massless constraints; estimated rotor inertias include attached hardware rather than a measured distributed coupling-rod model.

The print datum is (0, 0.14, -0.07) m. The segment fan has 50 mm radius at y=70 mm, and basket shift travel is estimated at 4 mm. This is taller than the former cosmetic casing; the renderer must be rebuilt around the mechanism. Platen radius remains the documented 18.8722 mm. Paper/ribbon thicknesses are estimated at 100/80 micrometers; the curved face envelope is 1.9 × 2.6 mm. The segment abutment is defined by a 12 mm bar-heel arm meeting a basket-mounted plane at nominal print alignment, so removing paper or ribbon does not let the bar pass through the machine.

Estimated rotor mass/inertia pairs are key lever 18 g / 45e-6 kg m², front link 4 g / 1e-6 kg m², typebar lever 5 g / 2e-6 kg m², and typebar 7 g / 18e-6 kg m². Inertia is about each hinge, including its COM offset. Return torsion rate is fitted per key length to approximately 350 N/m at the finger with 0.3 N preload. The virtual finger applies 4 + 4 f² N for force setting f, releases at the first forward stop, and has a 160 ms maximum force pulse. Damping is 6/s in the kinetic metric and forward restitution is 0.16; the felt stop is inelastic. These estimates require sensitivity/performance checks, not claims of measured touch or sound.

The action's two faces, cylinder and layer thicknesses determine contact in configuration space before stepping. A curved rectangular envelope bounds the character; the glyph stencil/ink relief remains a macroscopic approximation. Material paper coordinates use cylinder arc length. Whole-machine acceptance still requires renderer/stencil agreement, a causally driven ribbon carrier, carriage reaction coupling, and all user-facing tests.
